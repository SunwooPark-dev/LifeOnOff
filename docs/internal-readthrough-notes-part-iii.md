## Output contract
### Keep-strengths
- 1. **Title/subtitle promise stays visible across all four chapters.** Part III consistently turns “without losing your edge” into concrete ownership rules: Ch. 8 keeps meaning, specificity, and final send with the writer; Ch. 9 keeps truth and evidentiary threshold human-owned; Ch. 10 keeps tradeoffs and accepted pain with the leader; Ch. 11 keeps interpretation, ownership, and decision traceability with the human owner.
- 2. **The chapter-level must-prove claims mostly land with usable real-work scaffolding.** Ch. 8’s feature-cut memo shows AI helping with structure testing and tightening after the writer decides the hard thing to say; Ch. 9 clearly separates exploration from verification and adds a source ladder; Ch. 10 forces decision criteria, constraints, tradeoff tables, and premortems; Ch. 11 turns messy meetings into decisions / open questions / actions / interpretations instead of one smooth recap.
- 3. **Part III has a strong internal progression from individual craft to shared execution.** The sequence moves cleanly from writing (Ch. 8) to research (Ch. 9) to planning (Ch. 10) to coordination (Ch. 11), which makes the applications section feel like a practical bridge between the Part II operating model and the Part IV human-edge / future argument.

### Must-fix issues
- **P1 | Evidence:** Ch. 8–11 share almost the same rhetorical skeleton: domain-specific “used to fail…” opening, “wrong way” section, “right role/question,” numbered workflow, “real-work scenario,” “where AI helps most,” “where people get into trouble,” checklist, and “deeper point.” Across four consecutive applications chapters, that repetition makes Part III feel templated in exactly the way the packet warns against. **Revision action:** break the template in at least two chapters by cutting one repeated list section and varying the opening / scenario architecture so the applications feel observed rather than manufactured.
- **P1 | Evidence:** Ch. 11 drifts back into Ch. 10 territory instead of staying tightly inside its communication/coordination boundary. Step 2 in Ch. 11 centers a pre-meeting decision brief with context, tradeoffs, options, and decision needed, and the launch-scope scenario’s “Before the meeting” section repeats the same planning frame already handled in Ch. 10’s decision-definition, criteria, and tradeoff-table workflow. **Revision action:** recenter Ch. 11 on translation, recap integrity, ownership confirmation, and handoff mechanics after a decision frame already exists; trim or compress the planning apparatus that belongs to Ch. 10.
- **P2 | Evidence:** Ch. 9 explains the source ladder and uncertainty discipline well in the abstract, but the core enterprise-feature case does not dramatize them concretely enough. The scenario moves from defining the research job to gathering evidence to drafting the recommendation, yet it never shows which inputs sit on which rung of the ladder or what uncertainty remains explicit in the final recommendation. **Revision action:** add one concrete verification beat inside the case (for example, vendor claim vs. customer evidence vs. compliance input) and carry one explicit unresolved uncertainty into the recommendation language.

### Watch items
- **P2 | Evidence:** Part III’s examples cluster heavily around internal product / launch / leadership contexts: Ch. 8 uses a feature-cut strategy memo, Ch. 9 uses an enterprise-platform feature decision, Ch. 10 uses a quarterly launch review, and Ch. 11 uses a launch-scope coordination meeting. The logic works, but the application surface risks feeling narrower than the subtitle’s broader “think, work, and create” promise. **Revision action:** in a later case-diversity pass, swap or extend at least one scenario outside product-launch work or add a line that makes the transferability explicit.
- **P3 | Evidence:** Ch. 8 proves the workflow, but most of the chapter’s weight sits in frameworks and checklists rather than in sentence-level editorial demonstration. Beyond the single generic-vs-specific sentence pair and the short memo scenario, the chapter does not yet show much “on-the-page” revision texture. **Revision action:** if there is a later craft pass, add one additional paragraph-level before/after revision example so the writing chapter feels slightly more written-from-the-desk than taught-from-the-framework.
