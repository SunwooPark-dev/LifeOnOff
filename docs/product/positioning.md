# Product Positioning

## One-liner
An explainable decision-support workspace that helps people compare **2 to 5 personal daily-choice options** without pretending to predict the future or decide on the user's behalf.

## Target users
- Individuals making everyday but meaningful personal choices with visible tradeoffs
- People who want to compare several plausible options, not just be told what to do
- Users who value transparent reasoning, explicit assumptions, and clear uncertainty language

## Core use cases
- Compare 2 to 5 realistic options for a personal daily choice
- Surface tradeoffs, missing information, and which assumptions are driving the ordering
- Let the user confirm or adjust decision weights before any ranked recommendation appears
- Clarify what to validate next when evidence is weak or the result must be downgraded

## Value proposition
- Helps users think through a decision in a structured, inspectable way
- Separates facts, assumptions, inferences, and uncertainty instead of hiding them
- Preserves user authority by requiring explicit weight confirmation before final ranking
- Provides a recommendation summary only as advisory support, never as auto-action or mandate

## Product boundaries
- Personal daily choice MVP only
- Maximum 5 options per run
- No automatic action taking
- No professional-advice positioning
- No fabricated certainty when evidence is thin

## Non-goals
- Not fortune-telling or prediction
- Not a tool that makes the final decision for the user
- Not therapy, counseling, or professional advice
- Not an optimization engine that collapses a life choice into a magical single score
- Not a replacement for research, conversations, or judgment

## Messaging guardrails
- Position it as a decision-support system, explainable comparison workspace, or structured choice explorer
- Emphasize comparison, reflection, evidence, assumptions, and user-confirmed priorities
- Say it helps users explore likely tradeoffs, not forecast destiny
- Avoid language that implies certainty, authority, or hidden truth
- Avoid claims like “the app decides for you,” “predict your best future,” or “discover the right life automatically”
- Prefer language like “compare,” “inspect,” “clarify,” “weigh,” “explore,” and “reflect”

## Messaging examples
- Good: “Compare up to five options with explicit assumptions, evidence, and tradeoffs.”
- Good: “Clarify a personal choice before you commit, without pretending the system knows your future.”
- Good: “Confirm what matters to you first, then inspect a non-binding recommendation summary.”
- Bad: “Let AI tell you which path to choose.”
- Bad: “Predict the best future with one click.”
